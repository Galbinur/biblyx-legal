The Selfish Giant

Oscar Wilde · 1888 · O Gigante Egoísta

A2–B111 min de leitura1.652 palavrasDomínio público

Um gigante proíbe as crianças de brincar em seu belo jardim, e o inverno se instala por lá para sempre. Every afternoon, as they were coming from school, the children used to go and play in the Giant’s garden.

Toda tarde, quando voltavam da escola, as crianças costumavam ir brincar no jardim do Gigante.

It was a large lovely garden, with soft green grass. Here and there over the grass stood beautiful flowers like stars, and there were twelve peach-trees that in the spring-time broke out into delicate blossoms of pink and pearl, and in the autumn bore rich fruit. The birds sat on the trees and sang so sweetly that the children used to stop their games in order to listen to them. “How happy we are here!” they cried to each other.

Era um jardim grande e encantador, com uma grama verde e macia. Aqui e ali, sobre a grama, havia flores lindas como estrelas, e havia doze pessegueiros que, na primavera, desabrochavam em delicadas flores cor-de-rosa e pérola e, no outono, davam frutos fartos. Os pássaros pousavam nas árvores e cantavam tão docemente que as crianças paravam as brincadeiras para ouvi-los. “Como somos felizes aqui!”, gritavam umas para as outras.

One day the Giant came back. He had been to visit his friend the Cornish ogre, and had stayed with him for seven years. After the seven years were over he had said all that he had to say, for his conversation was limited, and he determined to return to his own castle. When he arrived he saw the children playing in the garden.

Um dia o Gigante voltou. Tinha ido visitar seu amigo, o ogro da Cornualha, e ficara com ele por sete anos. Passados os sete anos, já havia dito tudo o que tinha a dizer, pois sua conversa era limitada, e resolveu voltar para o seu próprio castelo. Ao chegar, viu as crianças brincando no jardim.

“What are you doing here?” he cried in a very gruff voice, and the children ran away.

“O que vocês estão fazendo aqui?”, gritou com uma voz muito rouca e áspera, e as crianças saíram correndo.

“My own garden is my own garden,” said the Giant; “any one can understand that, and I will allow nobody to play in it but myself.” So he built a high wall all round it, and put up a notice-board.

“Meu jardim é meu jardim”, disse o Gigante; “qualquer um entende isso, e não vou permitir que ninguém brinque nele além de mim.” Então construiu um muro alto em volta de todo o jardim e pendurou uma placa de aviso.

He was a very selfish Giant.

Era um Gigante muito egoísta.

The poor children had now nowhere to play. They tried to play on the road, but the road was very dusty and full of hard stones, and they did not like it. They used to wander round the high wall when their lessons were over, and talk about the beautiful garden inside. “How happy we were there,” they said to each other.

As pobres crianças agora não tinham onde brincar. Tentaram brincar na estrada, mas a estrada era muito empoeirada e cheia de pedras duras, e elas não gostaram. Quando as aulas terminavam, costumavam ficar rondando o muro alto e conversar sobre o belo jardim lá dentro. “Como éramos felizes lá!”, diziam umas para as outras.

Then the Spring came, and all over the country there were little blossoms and little birds. Only in the garden of the Selfish Giant it was still winter. The birds did not care to sing in it as there were no children, and the trees forgot to blossom. Once a beautiful flower put its head out from the grass, but when it saw the notice-board it was so sorry for the children that it slipped back into the ground again, and went off to sleep. The only people who were pleased were the Snow and the Frost. “Spring has forgotten this garden,” they cried, “so we will live here all the year round.” The Snow covered up the grass with her great white cloak, and the Frost painted all the trees silver. Then they invited the North Wind to stay with them, and he came. He was wrapped in furs, and he roared all day about the garden, and blew the chimney-pots down. “This is a delightful spot,” he said, “we must ask the Hail on a visit.” So the Hail came. Every day for three hours he rattled on the roof of the castle till he broke most of the slates, and then he ran round and round the garden as fast as he could go. He was dressed in grey, and his breath was like ice.

Então chegou a Primavera e, por todo o país, havia florezinhas e passarinhos. Só no jardim do Gigante Egoísta ainda era inverno. Os pássaros não queriam cantar ali, porque não havia crianças, e as árvores se esqueceram de florescer. Certa vez, uma linda flor pôs a cabeça para fora da grama, mas, quando viu a placa de aviso, ficou com tanta pena das crianças que voltou a se esconder na terra e adormeceu. Os únicos que ficaram contentes foram a Neve e a Geada. “A Primavera se esqueceu deste jardim”, gritaram, “então vamos morar aqui o ano inteiro.” A Neve cobriu a grama com seu grande manto branco, e a Geada pintou todas as árvores de prata. Depois convidaram o Vento Norte para ficar com elas, e ele veio. Estava envolto em peles e rugiu o dia inteiro pelo jardim, derrubando as chaminés. “Este lugar é delicioso”, disse ele, “precisamos chamar o Granizo para uma visita.” Então o Granizo veio. Todos os dias, por três horas, tamborilava no telhado do castelo até quebrar a maior parte das telhas, e depois corria em volta do jardim o mais depressa que podia. Vestia-se de cinza, e seu hálito era como gelo.

“I cannot understand why the Spring is so late in coming,” said the Selfish Giant, as he sat at the window and looked out at his cold white garden; “I hope there will be a change in the weather.”

“Não consigo entender por que a Primavera demora tanto a chegar”, disse o Gigante Egoísta, sentado à janela, olhando para o seu jardim frio e branco; “espero que o tempo mude.”

But the Spring never came, nor the Summer. The Autumn gave golden fruit to every garden, but to the Giant’s garden she gave none. “He is too selfish,” she said. So it was always Winter there, and the North Wind, and the Hail, and the Frost, and the Snow danced about through the trees.

Mas a Primavera nunca veio, nem o Verão. O Outono deu frutos dourados a todos os jardins, mas ao jardim do Gigante não deu nenhum. “Ele é egoísta demais”, disse ela. Assim, ali era sempre Inverno, e o Vento Norte, o Granizo, a Geada e a Neve dançavam por entre as árvores.

One morning the Giant was lying awake in bed when he heard some lovely music. It sounded so sweet to his ears that he thought it must be the King’s musicians passing by. It was really only a little linnet singing outside his window, but it was so long since he had heard a bird sing in his garden that it seemed to him to be the most beautiful music in the world. Then the Hail stopped dancing over his head, and the North Wind ceased roaring, and a delicious perfume came to him through the open casement. “I believe the Spring has come at last,” said the Giant; and he jumped out of bed and looked out.

Certa manhã, o Gigante estava deitado, acordado, na cama quando ouviu uma música linda. Soava tão doce aos seus ouvidos que ele pensou que fossem os músicos do Rei passando por ali. Na verdade, era apenas um pequeno pintassilgo cantando do lado de fora da janela, mas fazia tanto tempo que ele não ouvia um pássaro cantar em seu jardim que lhe pareceu a música mais bela do mundo. Então o Granizo parou de dançar sobre a sua cabeça, o Vento Norte deixou de rugir, e um perfume delicioso chegou até ele pela janela aberta. “Acho que a Primavera finalmente chegou”, disse o Gigante; e pulou da cama e olhou para fora.

What did he see?

O que ele viu?

He saw a most wonderful sight. Through a little hole in the wall the children had crept in, and they were sitting in the branches of the trees. In every tree that he could see there was a little child. And the trees were so glad to have the children back again that they had covered themselves with blossoms, and were waving their arms gently above the children’s heads. The birds were flying about and twittering with delight, and the flowers were looking up through the green grass and laughing. It was a lovely scene, only in one corner it was still winter. It was the farthest corner of the garden, and in it was standing a little boy. He was so small that he could not reach up to the branches of the tree, and he was wandering all round it, crying bitterly. The poor tree was still quite covered with frost and snow, and the North Wind was blowing and roaring above it. “Climb up! little boy,” said the Tree, and it bent its branches down as low as it could; but the boy was too tiny.

Viu uma cena maravilhosa. Por um buraquinho no muro, as crianças tinham entrado de mansinho e estavam sentadas nos galhos das árvores. Em cada árvore que ele podia ver, havia uma criança pequena. E as árvores estavam tão contentes de ter as crianças de volta que tinham se coberto de flores e balançavam os braços suavemente sobre a cabeça delas. Os pássaros voavam por todo lado, gorjeando de alegria, e as flores espiavam por entre a grama verde e riam. Era uma cena encantadora, só que, num canto, ainda era inverno. Era o canto mais distante do jardim, e nele estava um menininho. Era tão pequeno que não alcançava os galhos da árvore, e andava em volta dela, chorando amargamente. A pobre árvore ainda estava coberta de geada e neve, e o Vento Norte soprava e rugia por cima dela. “Suba, menininho!”, disse a Árvore, e curvou os galhos o mais baixo que pôde; mas o menino era pequeno demais.

And the Giant’s heart melted as he looked out. “How selfish I have been!” he said; “now I know why the Spring would not come here. I will put that poor little boy on the top of the tree, and then I will knock down the wall, and my garden shall be the children’s playground for ever and ever.” He was really very sorry for what he had done.

E o coração do Gigante se derreteu ao olhar para fora. “Como fui egoísta!”, disse ele; “agora sei por que a Primavera não quis vir aqui. Vou colocar aquele pobre menininho no alto da árvore, depois vou derrubar o muro, e o meu jardim será o parquinho das crianças para todo o sempre.” Ele estava realmente muito arrependido do que tinha feito.

So he crept downstairs and opened the front door quite softly, and went out into the garden. But when the children saw him they were so frightened that they all ran away, and the garden became winter again. Only the little boy did not run, for his eyes were so full of tears that he did not see the Giant coming. And the Giant stole up behind him and took him gently in his hand, and put him up into the tree. And the tree broke at once into blossom, and the birds came and sang on it, and the little boy stretched out his two arms and flung them round the Giant’s neck, and kissed him. And the other children, when they saw that the Giant was not wicked any longer, came running back, and with them came the Spring. “It is your garden now, little children,” said the Giant, and he took a great axe and knocked down the wall. And when the people were going to market at twelve o’clock they found the Giant playing with the children in the most beautiful garden they had ever seen.

Então desceu a escada na ponta dos pés, abriu a porta da frente bem de mansinho e saiu para o jardim. Mas, quando as crianças o viram, ficaram tão assustadas que fugiram todas, e o jardim voltou a ser inverno. Só o menininho não fugiu, pois seus olhos estavam tão cheios de lágrimas que ele não viu o Gigante chegando. E o Gigante se aproximou por trás dele, pegou-o com delicadeza na mão e o colocou na árvore. A árvore floresceu na mesma hora, os pássaros vieram cantar nela, e o menininho estendeu os dois bracinhos, abraçou o pescoço do Gigante e o beijou. E as outras crianças, quando viram que o Gigante não era mais mau, voltaram correndo, e com elas veio a Primavera. “O jardim agora é de vocês, crianças”, disse o Gigante, e pegou um grande machado e derrubou o muro. E, quando as pessoas iam ao mercado ao meio-dia, encontraram o Gigante brincando com as crianças no jardim mais lindo que já tinham visto.

All day long they played, and in the evening they came to the Giant to bid him good-bye.

Brincaram o dia inteiro e, ao anoitecer, foram até o Gigante para se despedir dele.

“But where is your little companion?” he said: “the boy I put into the tree.” The Giant loved him the best because he had kissed him.

“Mas onde está o seu amiguinho?”, perguntou ele: “o menino que eu coloquei na árvore.” O Gigante gostava mais dele porque o menino o havia beijado.

“We don’t know,” answered the children; “he has gone away.”

“Não sabemos”, responderam as crianças; “ele foi embora.”

“You must tell him to be sure and come here to-morrow,” said the Giant. But the children said that they did not know where he lived, and had never seen him before; and the Giant felt very sad.

“Digam a ele que não deixe de vir aqui amanhã”, disse o Gigante. Mas as crianças disseram que não sabiam onde ele morava e que nunca o tinham visto antes; e o Gigante ficou muito triste.

Every afternoon, when school was over, the children came and played with the Giant. But the little boy whom the Giant loved was never seen again. The Giant was very kind to all the children, yet he longed for his first little friend, and often spoke of him. “How I would like to see him!” he used to say.

Toda tarde, quando a aula terminava, as crianças vinham brincar com o Gigante. Mas o menininho que o Gigante amava nunca mais foi visto. O Gigante era muito bondoso com todas as crianças, mas sentia saudade do seu primeiro amiguinho e falava dele com frequência. “Como eu gostaria de vê-lo!”, costumava dizer.

Years went over, and the Giant grew very old and feeble. He could not play about any more, so he sat in a huge armchair, and watched the children at their games, and admired his garden. “I have many beautiful flowers,” he said; “but the children are the most beautiful flowers of all.”

Os anos se passaram, e o Gigante ficou muito velho e fraco. Já não podia brincar, por isso se sentava numa enorme poltrona, observava as crianças em suas brincadeiras e admirava o seu jardim. “Tenho muitas flores lindas”, dizia ele; “mas as crianças são as flores mais lindas de todas.”

One winter morning he looked out of his window as he was dressing. He did not hate the Winter now, for he knew that it was merely the Spring asleep, and that the flowers were resting.

Numa manhã de inverno, ele olhou pela janela enquanto se vestia. Já não odiava o Inverno, pois sabia que ele era apenas a Primavera dormindo e que as flores estavam descansando.

Suddenly he rubbed his eyes in wonder, and looked and looked. It certainly was a marvellous sight. In the farthest corner of the garden was a tree quite covered with lovely white blossoms. Its branches were all golden, and silver fruit hung down from them, and underneath it stood the little boy he had loved.

De repente, esfregou os olhos de espanto e ficou olhando e olhando. Era sem dúvida uma visão maravilhosa. No canto mais distante do jardim havia uma árvore toda coberta de lindas flores brancas. Seus galhos eram todos dourados, frutos de prata pendiam deles, e embaixo dela estava o menininho que ele amara.

Downstairs ran the Giant in great joy, and out into the garden. He hastened across the grass, and came near to the child. And when he came quite close his face grew red with anger, and he said, “Who hath dared to wound thee?” For on the palms of the child’s hands were the prints of two nails, and the prints of two nails were on the little feet.

Lá embaixo correu o Gigante, cheio de alegria, e saiu para o jardim. Atravessou a grama depressa e chegou perto da criança. E, quando chegou bem perto, seu rosto ficou vermelho de raiva, e ele disse: “Quem ousou ferir-te?” Pois nas palmas das mãos da criança havia as marcas de dois pregos, e as marcas de dois pregos estavam nos pezinhos.

“Who hath dared to wound thee?” cried the Giant; “tell me, that I may take my big sword and slay him.”

“Quem ousou ferir-te?”, gritou o Gigante; “diz-me, para que eu pegue a minha grande espada e o mate.”

“Nay!” answered the child; “but these are the wounds of Love.”

“Não!”, respondeu a criança; “estas são as feridas do Amor.”

“Who art thou?” said the Giant, and a strange awe fell on him, and he knelt before the little child.

“Quem és tu?”, disse o Gigante, e um estranho temor reverente o dominou, e ele se ajoelhou diante da criança.

And the child smiled on the Giant, and said to him, “You let me play once in your garden, to-day you shall come with me to my garden, which is Paradise.”

E a criança sorriu para o Gigante e disse: “Você me deixou brincar uma vez no seu jardim; hoje você virá comigo para o meu jardim, que é o Paraíso.”

And when the children ran in that afternoon, they found the Giant lying dead under the tree, all covered with white blossoms.

E, quando as crianças entraram correndo naquela tarde, encontraram o Gigante morto debaixo da árvore, todo coberto de flores brancas.
