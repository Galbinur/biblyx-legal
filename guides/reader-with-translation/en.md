Most e-book readers have a dictionary. You long-press a word, a box opens with six meanings, and you pick one. That works for a native speaker who meets one unknown word per chapter. For a language learner it fails in a specific way: the sixth unknown word on a page is where the reading stops. Biblyx is a reading app for English and nine other languages, built around that problem. You open your own EPUB, PDF or TXT file, tap a word, and get the meaning it has in that sentence, then a flashcard that keeps the sentence. The rest of the app exists to make that loop fast enough to survive a whole book.

## What "translation" means here

A tap on a word doesn't open a dictionary page. The AI reads the sentence you are in and returns one meaning, the one that fits there, with the word's part of speech, its base form and an estimated CEFR level from A1 to C2. When the word is part of an idiom or expression ("make up", "take off", "call it a day"), you see both the expression and the word on its own, and you can save either as a card. German, Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese nouns come with their article; compound words are split into their parts. If a whole sentence is the problem, select the passage and translate it as a block. A speaker button reads the word with your phone's text-to-speech voice.

Meanings appear in your interface language. Biblyx translates books in ten languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Russian, Polish, Portuguese, Turkish and Azerbaijani; Swedish, Dutch, Ukrainian and Tajik are in beta. A book in any other language still opens and reads normally, without the tap-to-translate.

## Which files open

- **EPUB**, the format of Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks and most DRM-free shops.
- **TXT**, plain text of any size.
- **PDF with real text.** Scanned PDFs are photos of pages; no reader can tap a word in a photo, and Biblyx tells you when you open one.
- **Pasted text.** Since version 1.3.0 you can paste an article, a story or a long message and read it like a book, with the same tap-to-translate and the same cards.

Files open from the app's "+" button or straight from a file manager, a browser download or an email attachment through "Open with Biblyx". A table of contents takes you to any chapter in EPUB, TXT and PDF books; the reader has search, bookmarks and highlights in four colors, a light and a dark theme, and an auto-scroll mode. If your library is empty, 29 short stories and tales in ten languages are already there, so the first tap takes under a minute.

Locked books are the one honest limitation: books bought in Kindle or Apple Books, or borrowed through a library app, are locked to those apps and won't open in Biblyx or in any other reader.

## From a tapped word to a flashcard

One more tap saves the word to Word Lab together with the sentence it came from, and you can add a note. The card comes back for review after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, then at longer intervals, the schedule most spaced-repetition apps use. Reviews are sized for a phone: 10, 20 or 30 cards, an undo arrow for a mistaken answer, a button for "I already know this", and a quick multiple-choice test for the words you keep getting wrong, which the app marks as struggling. You can review only your saved expressions, or only the words from one book.

Nothing is locked in. Word Lab exports your words as an Anki deck (.apkg, with ready cards and pronunciation) or as a CSV file for a spreadsheet. Only the words go out, never the text of your books.

## Offline, account, cost

Reading and reviewing cards work offline. Looking up a new word or translating a passage needs an internet connection, because the meaning is computed for your sentence rather than read from a file. No account is needed to read and translate; signing in with email or Google syncs saved words, review progress, bookmarks and highlights between devices. Book files stay on your device and are never uploaded.

Reading, Word Lab, export, reminders and sync need no subscription, and there are no ads. AI translations use a daily allowance; Premium removes the daily limit (fair use applies), with a monthly, six-month or yearly plan. The app is on Android through Google Play; an iPhone version is planned, with no date yet.

## Who it is not for

A reader with translation is a tool for people who already read, or want to. It won't teach you the alphabet or your first hundred words; a course or a phrasebook does that better. It won't read a scanned PDF. It has no lessons, no streak to protect and no leaderboard. If you have a book you want to finish in a language you half know, it is for you.

## Starting in five minutes

1. Install Biblyx and choose the language you learn and your own language.
2. Open a story from the starter shelf, or add your own EPUB, PDF or TXT with "+".
3. Tap a word you don't know. Read its meaning in the sentence. Save it.
4. The next day, review the cards that are due; it takes two or three minutes.
5. Keep reading. That is the whole idea: **Learn languages by reading your own books**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can it translate a whole page?

Select any passage and translate it as a block. Word by word is the default, because a learner remembers a word met in a sentence better than a translated page.

### Does the PDF reader work with textbooks?

Yes, if the PDF has real text: tap any word, and use the outline to jump between chapters. Scanned textbooks don't work.

### Which language should my interface be in?

Your own language. Meanings appear in the interface language, so a Turkish speaker reading an English book sees Turkish meanings. If you read a book written in your interface language, you choose which language its words are translated into.

### Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. Biblyx is on Android through Google Play; an iOS version is planned, with no date.

### How is this different from Kindle's dictionary or Google Translate?

Kindle gives dictionary entries, one at a time, and keeps nothing. Google Translate turns the page into your language, so you end up reading your language instead of the one you learn. Biblyx gives one meaning fitted to the sentence, keeps it as a card with that sentence, and brings it back for review.
