English short stories

Three classic stories, complete and free to read, from a fairy tale to a crime told by a madman. Underlined words open a short meaning in simple English, and each story ends with its word list. The texts are public domain.

How to read: tap an underlined word to see what it means here. Read each paragraph through first and look words up only when they stop you.

The Selfish Giant

Oscar Wilde · 1888

A2–B111 min read1,652 wordsPublic domain

A giant builds a wall to keep children out of his garden, and winter never leaves it. Oscar Wilde's fairy tale uses short sentences and a lot of repetition, so it is a gentle first story at A2–B1. A few old words such as hath and thee are explained.

The Gift of the Magi

O. Henry · 1905

B114 min read2,057 wordsPublic domain

On Christmas Eve, Della has one dollar and eighty-seven cents to buy a present for her husband Jim. O. Henry's famous story is short and full of dialogue, a good B1 read, with the 1900s words explained as you go.

The Tell-Tale Heart

Edgar Allan Poe · 1843

B214 min read2,076 wordsPublic domain

A man insists he is not mad while he tells how an old man's pale eye drove him to murder. Poe's short, breathless sentences make it readable at B2, and the old literary words are explained in simple English.
